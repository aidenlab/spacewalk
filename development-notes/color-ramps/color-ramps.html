Sequential Color Ramps
for Population Data

Single-hue and multi-hue ramps suited to continuous population density or count mapping. All options are perceptually ordered — lighter values encode lower populations, darker values encode higher.

lowhigh
YlOrRd
Yellow → Orange → Red. The canonical ramp for population density maps — intuitive (hot = dense), high contrast at the upper end where data is most interesting. Used by the U.S. Census, UN, and nearly every major atlas.

Viridis
Deep purple → teal → yellow. Designed by matplotlib researchers to be perceptually uniform in both color and grayscale. Excellent for print reproduction and accessibility. Increasingly the scientific standard for sequential data.
Colorblind-safe Grayscale-safe Perceptually uniform matplotlib / D3
lowhigh
Plasma
Deep blue-violet → magenta → yellow. A perceptually uniform sibling of Viridis with warmer, higher-energy tones. More dramatic contrast and visual punch — well-suited to presentations and digital displays. Slightly less common, which helps your figures stand out.
Colorblind-safe Perceptually uniform Not grayscale-safe matplotlib / D3
lowhigh
YlGnBu
Yellow → green → blue. A cool-toned counterpart to YlOrRd — good when overlaying on warm basemaps (terrain, satellite). Feels geographically natural and is often used for population maps in academic journals. The blue endpoint reads as "dense" without alarm-connotations of red.
Colorblind-safe Print-safe Perceptually ordered ColorBrewer
lowhigh
Blues
Near-white → deep navy. Single-hue, maximally clean. The low end nearly disappears into a white background, which can be elegant for sparse datasets or inset maps. Very neutral — avoids implying alarm or heat — which is appropriate when population is being presented without policy framing.
Colorblind-safe Print-safe Neutral tone ColorBrewer
lowhigh
Inferno
Black → deep purple → orange → pale yellow. Dramatically high-contrast, ideal for dark-background maps and screen presentations. Low population values recede into black, making dense urban cores blazingly visible. A striking choice for city-scale or night-light style population maps.
